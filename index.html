How to record it

Car, engine off — best free vocal booth you own. Kill the radio and anything naming a place.
Phone at chin level, 6–8 inches, slightly off to the side of your mouth
Read twice, put it down, talk. Don't read while recording — it flattens into recital.
Use take two. Take one is fumbling, take five is a performance.
Fumble? Don't restart — say it again and keep it. That is the take.
Say the hardest sentence quieter. You will anyway, if you're not reading.
No cleanup after. No noise gate, no de-breathing. The breaths are the proof.
Ten seconds of silence at the end — keep it. Room tone makes edits invisible.

What to point the camera at

One unbroken 40-second handheld shot beats five perfect clips — the unbroken take is the thing that can't be assembled. Don't tidy the frame first; the clutter is the evidence. Shoot at the hour the video is about.

Best options, in order: hands on the steering wheel in dashboard glow · windshield POV of whatever's really there · the corner that might become the nursery, at 11pm · your phone in your hand in a dark room.

Rule 4 is a camera rule. You're hunting the corners, backgrounds and reflections, not the story.

Paper — mail, bills, receipts with store names, prescription labels, envelopes

The four rules — nothing ships that fails these

1 · The pregnancy is fair game. Everyone who matters knows. Real dates, real milestones, the day counter runs.

2 · She exists, never described. "She/we" is fine. No name, appearance, personality, dialogue, symptoms. No second character — every video is told from inside your own head.

3 · Money in shapes, never figures. "Rent eats about a third." No real numbers on screen, in voice, or in captions — including this channel's revenue, including zero.

4 · No job, employer, industry, city. "Work" is a flat noun and a wall of time. This is a camera rule too — see the frame check.

Plus the fifth check that isn't a privacy rule: every claim must be actually true of you. That's the rule the old library broke.

Why there were no views — the short version

First problem: zero engagement signal. 0 comments, 0 saves, 0 shares account-wide. Platforms show a video to more people only when the first few DO something. Views are downstream.

Second problem: repetition. Every video restated the premise instead of advancing a story — video 49 contained nothing video 1 didn't, so there was never a reason to follow. The fix is structural: the serial. Day counter + countdown means the content can't repeat, because the number moves.

Third: the packaging read as the category people swipe past. "5-Step Plan," "Why Most Dads Fail" — guru grammar. Replaced by what actually happened.

Humanize — the five that matter most

1 · Voice is the biggest lever. Real voice over mediocre footage reads as real; synthetic voice over perfect footage reads as an ad.

2 · Don't clean the audio. Breath, clicks, swallows — TTS strips them, so leaving them in is the loudest proof of a person.

3 · One useless true detail per video. The line with no purpose is the line that proves the place is real.

4 · Never fake the imperfection. Fake ums and fake shake read worse than clean AI. People detect performed authenticity faster than they detect AI.

5 · Disclose the avatar, in your own words, once. It kills the uncanny feeling, makes "getting caught" impossible, and is itself the differentiator.
